Privacy Policy

Buckshot for Mac · Last updated October 14, 2026

Short version: Buckshot runs on your Mac and has no ads. Your contacts, messages and files are never sent to the developer or to anyone other than the people you choose to message. Buckshot's small server only keeps your account (your email address and plan), a count of how many send-outs you make each week, optional email open tracking and anonymous usage counts, all described below.

What Buckshot stores, and where

  • Contacts and lists you enter (names, phone numbers, email addresses), drafts, and send history (who you sent which files to, and when). These are saved only on your Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/Buckshot.
  • Your files are never uploaded anywhere by Buckshot except as attachments to the messages you send. For iMessage, Buckshot makes a temporary copy for the Messages app and permanently deletes it shortly after sending.
  • Apple Contacts (optional): if you choose Import from Apple Contacts, Buckshot reads the names, phone numbers and email addresses in your Mac's Contacts app, with your permission, and adds the people you pick to Buckshot's contacts on your Mac. It never changes anything in the Contacts app and never sends your contacts anywhere.
  • Your email sign-in (a Google sign-in token, or an app password for other providers) is stored in your Mac's Keychain, never in a file.

Google user data

If you sign in with Google to send email from Gmail, Buckshot requests these permissions:

  • Send email on your behalf (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.send): used only to send the messages and attachments you write in Buckshot, to the recipients you pick, when you press Send.
  • Your name and email address (openid, email, profile): used to show which account you're sending from, to put your name in the From line, and as your Buckshot account. Buckshot's server receives your email address (and Google's proof that it's yours) so it can look up your plan. See “Your Buckshot account” below.
  • Files Buckshot creates in your Google Drive (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file): used only when your files are too big to attach to an email (over Gmail's 25 MB limit). Buckshot uploads those files to a “Buckshot” folder in your own Drive, shares them so anyone with the link can view them, and puts the link in the email, the same way Gmail handles large attachments. This permission only covers files Buckshot itself created: Buckshot can't see, open or change any other file in your Drive. The uploaded files stay in your Drive until you delete them.

Buckshot does not read, search, download, change or delete any of your email, contacts or other Google data. It does not store the content of your Gmail account. Apart from your email address being used as your Buckshot account, your Google data is never sent to the developer. It is never sold, never used for advertising, and never shared with or transferred to anyone, except that the messages you send go to the recipients you chose, through Google.

Buckshot's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Your Buckshot account and plan

Buckshot uses the Google account you sign in with as your Buckshot account; there's no separate password. Buckshot's server stores:

  • Your email address and your plan (free, trial, subscription or lifetime, and when it renews or ends).
  • How many send-outs you've made each week, and how many people each went to, so the free plan's limits can be applied. Only the numbers are stored: never who you sent to, what you wrote, or which files.

Payments are handled by our reseller and merchant of record, Paddle. Paddle collects your payment details under its own privacy policy; Buckshot never sees your card number. Paddle tells Buckshot's server which email address bought which plan, and when a subscription renews, changes or ends.

We keep this information while you have a Buckshot account and delete it on request (see Contact below).

iMessage

iMessages are sent by the Messages app on your Mac, using your own Apple account. Buckshot asks macOS for permission to control Messages for this purpose only.

Delivered / Read (optional, off by default): if you turn this on, Buckshot reads the delivered and read times of the iMessages it sent, from Messages' own records on your Mac (read-only). macOS requires Full Disk Access for this. Buckshot doesn't read your conversations, and this information never leaves your Mac.

Scheduled send-outs are stored on your Mac with copies of their files, which are permanently deleted after they're sent or cancelled.

Email open tracking (optional)

If you turn on Track opens for a send-out, each email carries a tiny invisible image from Buckshot's server. When the recipient's mail app loads it, the server stores a random code, the time, and the mail app's name (its "user agent"), for up to 120 days, so Buckshot can show you "Opened". No names, email addresses or message contents are stored. Only your copy of Buckshot can read your opens.

Anonymous usage counts

To see how many people use Buckshot, it sends two anonymous counts to Buckshot's server: once a day "this Mac used Buckshot", and after each send-out how many people it went to. The only identifier is a random number made on your Mac. No names, email addresses, contacts, messages or files are ever included. You can turn this off in Settings ▸ Sending & Files ▸ Share anonymous usage counts.

Removing your data

  • Disconnect Google: in Buckshot, open Settings ▸ Email ▸ Sign Out. This deletes the sign-in token from your Keychain. You can also remove Buckshot's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Delete your account: email us (below) from the address you sign in with, and we'll delete your account and plan from Buckshot's server.
  • Delete everything else: quit Buckshot and delete the folder ~/Library/Application Support/Buckshot, then delete the app.

Children

Buckshot isn't directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect their information.
